02 / The mechanism

Speed in. Slower rotation out.

The motor turns an eccentric input. That offset moves the center of each disk around the fixed outer roller ring; the output pins collect the disks’ slower rotation.

The V1 reduction

Each disk has 15 lobes working against 16 outer rollers.

15 ÷ (16 − 15) = 15:1

Reduction magnitude = disk lobes ÷ (outer rollers − disk lobes). That means fifteen turns of the input for one revolution of the output.

What the motor rating means

A reference, not a test result

The STEPPERONLINE motor is rated at 2.4 N·m holding torque. Holding torque describes an energized, stationary motor under its rated conditions.

2.4 × 15 = 36 N·m is an ideal holding-based reference. It is not a measured output torque or a promise of moving torque; driver settings, speed, losses and the printed mechanism all matter.

04 / Assembly notes

How the stack comes together

The sequence I worked through in V1. Use the included print project and parts list, and confirm the purchased hardware and mating fits before assembly.

  1. Match the model to the hardware

    Start with the included 3MF and its printed-parts list: 15 lobes per disk and 16 outer rollers. Check the bearing, pin and shaft interfaces against your actual hardware; the 3MF is a print layout, not an assembled CAD model.

  2. Print the custom parts

    The saved set has 26 pieces on three plates. Its slice used a P1S / 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, 15% infill, two walls and no supports. Review the project for your own printer; the saved estimate is 296.56 g.

  3. Check the bearing fits

    Measure the bearing seats and try the fits before building the full stack. The downloads give mesh dimensions, not a validated press-fit specification. Confirm actual bearing sizes and clearances; forcing a tight printed fit can crack the part.

  4. Build the outer roller ring

    Install the sixteen roller assemblies in the housing with their bearings and spacing hardware. Confirm the individual roller stack, purchased bearing dimensions and fastener lengths against the actual parts.

  5. Connect the motor input

    The connection evolved from a printed friction fit to inserts/set screws, then a metal coupler. V1 uses an 8 mm → 6 mm coupler; verify alignment and access to its screws.

  6. Add the two disks and spacers

    Confirm the disks’ eccentric phasing and stack order against the build. The saved set has sixteen spacer instances with 6 mm mesh height. Align the output holes and check that tightening does not squeeze the moving parts together.

  7. Fit the output carrier

    The output pins pass through the larger disk holes and collect the slower rotation. Check clearance and free movement as the assembly is tightened; purchased-pin dimensions and the final mating fits still need confirmation.

  8. Bring up the controls

    Use the included matching firmware, USB panel and wiring guide. Record the actual driver current, microsteps and supply voltage before interpreting commanded motion or a scale reading.

05 / Controls

A simple bench-control setup

The current setup uses the STM32 Nucleo-L476RG, an EASON TB6600 and a local USB browser panel.

What it does

Jog, hold and load slowly

  • Hold Forward or Reverse to jog; release to stop and hold.
  • Run a slower loading move while observing the scale.
  • Use Stop & Hold or Release as appropriate for the supported mechanism.
  • Show commanded pulse position, not encoder-measured position.

The scale is not a feedback sensor for the controller. The panel does not measure torque or automatically correct for friction or arm gravity.

Entered moment arm: 160 mm
Visible software baseline: 0 g

How to read those numbers

Preliminary, not maximum torque

These are calculated scale-contact moments. Arm gravity and friction were not corrected, and the perpendicular moment arm was not independently verified.

The printed stand was a quick support and I held the motor down because I didn’t have proper clamps. Actual test supply, current, microsteps and the limiting condition were not fully recorded.

This check does not establish maximum output torque or gearbox efficiency. Better restraint, geometry and repeatable test conditions are the next step.
